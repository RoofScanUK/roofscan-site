What to photograph

The report writer builds the whole report from your photos and never visits the property. If a roof element is not in your photos, it does not appear in the report. Use this list on every job.

Before you leave
  • Fly the whole roof, not just the parts that look bad. Clean areas need photos too, because "no issues found" has to be shown.
  • Shoot in one continuous order (see below). The report generator keeps photos in the order you took them and tags roof areas as it goes.
  • Keep location services switched on so each photo carries its GPS position.
  • Do not edit, crop, filter or rename photos.

Shooting order

Work round the property the same way every time, so the writer can follow it.

  • 1. Address shot. The front of the property from the street, showing the whole building and house number if visible.
  • 2. Overview. One high, wide shot of the whole roof from each side, so the writer can see how the roof fits together.
  • 3. Front slope. Straight-down photos covering the full slope in overlapping passes, then one angled shot along it.
  • 4. Rear slope. The same as the front.
  • 5. Side slopes and any extensions, porches, bays or outbuildings. Treat each separate roof as its own set.

Close-ups on every roof

Take at least one clear, sharp photo of each of these where they exist. Move in until the detail fills most of the frame.

  • Ridge and hips. Ridge tiles, mortar or dry fix, and the ends of each run.
  • Valleys. Lead or GRP lining, and any debris sitting in them.
  • Chimneys. All four sides, the flashing and any lead, the pots, the flaunching on top, and the pointing.
  • Flashings and abutments. Where the roof meets a wall, a dormer, a vent or a neighbour.
  • Verges and eaves. The edge tiles, mortar, fascias, soffits and bargeboards.
  • Gutters and downpipes. Joints, brackets, blockages and vegetation.
  • Vents, flues, soil pipes, aerials and roof lights. Including the seal round each one.
  • Solar panels, if fitted. The array, its fixings, and the roof around the edges.
  • Flat roof sections. The surface, upstands, drains and any ponding water.

Anything that looks wrong

Take three photos of every defect, in this order, so it can be understood and found again:

  • A wide shot showing where it is on the roof.
  • A medium shot showing the area around it.
  • A close-up of the damage itself.

Typical things to look for: slipped, cracked or missing tiles or slates, moss and lichen growth, lifted or cracked ridge mortar, failed or lifted flashing, sagging or dips, damaged gutters, and ponding on flat roofs. Photograph what you see. Do not guess at causes. The writer and the report deal with that.

Photo quality

  • Sharp and in focus. If it is blurry, take it again before you land.
  • Avoid shooting straight into the sun. Move round the roof if glare hides the surface.
  • Keep the camera level on flat shots, and keep the horizon level on angled shots.
  • Take extra photos. Storage is cheap, and a second trip is not.

Check before you leave site

  • Scroll through the photos on your phone or controller. Every item above is covered.
  • Nothing is blurred, and no photo is missing for a defect you saw.
  • Roughly how many photos, and in order. A normal house needs well over 100.

Uploading

Put all the photos into one shared folder (Google Drive, Dropbox or OneDrive) and set sharing to "anyone with the link can view". Paste that link into the job card on the jobs board and press Mark scan done. The button stays locked until a link is in.
