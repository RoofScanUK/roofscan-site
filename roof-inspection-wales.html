Roofs in Wales

Wales roofed much of the world with its slate, and its own homes still show it. Valley terraces, chapels and stone farmhouses sit in some of the wettest, windiest spots in the UK.

  • Welsh slate on valley terraces and town houses
  • Stone farmhouses and cottages with slate or stone tile roofs
  • Coastal homes exposed to Atlantic weather
  • Modern estates with concrete tiles around Cardiff, Newport and Swansea

What we look for

Slate lasts a long time, but the nails holding it don't, so slipped slates are the classic Welsh roof problem. We also look at ridges, chimneys and valleys. A drone photographs steep and terraced roofs without scaffolding.

Who books a roof inspection

Buying a house: check the roof before you exchange and use the report in your negotiation. After a storm: photos you can send to your insurer or roofer. Landlords: a dated record of the roof's condition for planned maintenance. Businesses: a check of shop, office or warehouse roofs with no disruption.
