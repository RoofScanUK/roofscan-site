Roofs in Portsmouth

Portsmouth is one of the most densely populated cities in the UK, and much of it is Victorian terraced streets in areas such as Fratton, Southsea and North End. Southsea has several conservation areas, including Owen's Southsea and The Terraces, and Historic England listings for Southsea terraces commonly record Welsh slate roofs.

  • Victorian terraces with Welsh slate and shared chimney stacks
  • Slate mansard roofs on some of Southsea's older terraces
  • Loft conversions and box dormers added to terraced houses
  • Homes on the Southsea seafront, open to wind off the Solent

What we look for

On a terrace, the stack you share with next door, slipped slates and the valley gutter at the back are the usual places to check. We also photograph dormers and loft conversions, where new roof meets old, and the flashings on homes facing the sea.

Why a drone

Mid-terrace houses often have no rear access for a ladder, and a scaffold on a narrow street needs space and permission. A drone sees the front and back slopes from the air in one visit.

Can you fly near the naval base?

Military sites such as the naval base can have airspace limits, and some addresses close to them cannot be flown. Before each flight the pilot checks the airspace for your address, and if we cannot fly we tell you.

Can you check a house with a loft conversion or dormer?

Yes. We photograph the dormer, its flat roof and the flashings where it joins the main roof, and rate each part in the report.
