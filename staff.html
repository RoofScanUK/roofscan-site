Flying — capture the roof
0 of 8
▾
Front elevation
Hover level with the roofline, ~10-15m back, slow steady pass left to right. Include the full front slope and gutter line in frame — this is the "cover shot" for the report.
Rear elevation
Same height and distance as the front pass. Check gardens/neighbours for anyone in frame before flying over.
Both side elevations
One pass per side, perpendicular to the wall. These catch valleys and hip ends the front/rear shots miss.
Every chimney — close-up
One shot per stack, close enough to see individual pots, flaunching/mortar cracks, and the chimney-to-roof flashing seal. Circle it if you can — flashing failures often show up on the side you'd otherwise miss.
Ridge line — full length
Slow pass directly along the ridge, close enough to spot slipped/cracked ridge tiles or open joints.
All flashings and valleys
Close-up on every roof-to-wall junction, valley, and any skylight/vent — these are the most common leak points and easy to under-shoot from a distance.
Guttering — full run
Follow every gutter run close enough to see sagging, blockages, or missing sections, not just from the elevation shots.
Any visible defect — extra close-up
Slipped/missing tiles, moss buildup, cracked sections, staining. If you spot something during the fly-through, stop and get a dedicated close shot of it — don't rely on it appearing in a wider pass.
Top-down overview of the full roof
Straight-down, high enough to fit the entire roof (all slopes) in one frame. This is the shot the area/footprint measurement and the report's overview image both rely on.
Angled 3/4 overview
A higher, angled shot showing the roof in context with the rest of the property — gives the client a "whole picture" shot the straight-down one doesn't.
Review footage on phone before landing
Check every elevation, chimney, and defect shot is sharp and in frame. Reshoot anything blurry or cut off now — going back up later means a second trip.

🧍
Can't get 50m clear of people (busy street etc): This is a hard limit, not a judgement call — don't fly through it. In order of preference: (1) ask people nearby to step back 50m while you fly that pass, (2) change your flight path or take-off point so the drone itself stays 50m clear even if people are on the street, (3) use a spotter or briefly cordon the pavement under your flight path, (4) fly at a quieter time. If none of that gets you to 50m, reschedule the job — don't fly anyway.

Client questions — what to say
Clients sometimes ask these on the doorstep. Give a calm, canned answer — don't improvise.
"Is this legal?"▾
Yes. We're a CAA-registered drone operator with a visible Operator ID on the aircraft, and we're fully insured through Flock Cover. We only ever film roofs — never interiors or gardens beyond what's needed to reach the roofline.
"Will you film my neighbours?"▾
No. The flight path stays directly over your property and roofline. We're not looking into neighbouring gardens or windows — the camera is angled at the roof, not sideways.
"Do I need to tell my neighbours?"▾
You're not legally required to, but it's a nice courtesy if a drone flying nearby might surprise them — entirely up to you.
"How long will this take?"▾
Usually 15–30 minutes on site depending on the size and complexity of the roof.
"What if it's too windy or starts raining?"▾
Safety comes first — if conditions aren't safe to fly we'll reschedule at no extra cost. We'd rather come back than risk a bad flight.
"Can I watch?"▾
Of course, just from a safe distance back from the take-off/landing area. Happy to explain what we're capturing as we go.
"What happens to my photos/data?"▾
They're stored securely and used only to build your report. We don't share or publish them without your permission.
🤐
What not to say: don't guarantee outcomes ("you'll definitely need a new roof"), don't give structural or legal advice beyond what's in the report, don't badmouth other roofers or trades, and don't negotiate or change the price on site — refer pricing questions to Takhy.
😬
If a client complains: stay calm and polite. Don't admit fault or promise a refund on the spot. Note down exactly what they said, then tell Takhy as soon as possible so it can be handled properly.
