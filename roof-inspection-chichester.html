Roofs in Chichester

Chichester is a cathedral city with Georgian and Victorian houses and many listed buildings in the centre. In the villages around it, Chichester District Council's conservation area appraisals describe flint walls and handmade clay peg tiles in places such as Oving and West Wittering, natural slate on some houses in Bosham, Donnington and Selsey, and some thatched roofs.

  • Handmade clay peg tiles on flint cottages in the villages
  • Georgian and Victorian town houses in the city centre
  • Natural slate on some older houses in Bosham, Donnington and Selsey
  • Thatched cottages in parts of the district

What we look for

Clay peg tiles can slip when their pegs or battens fail, so we look for gaps and slipped tiles across each slope. We also photograph lead valleys, flint parapets and chimneys. On a thatched roof we photograph the ridge and the surface, but we do not measure thatch depth or judge how long it will last, and the report says so.

Why a drone

Many roofs around Chichester are old, steep or listed, and nobody wants a ladder resting on fragile clay tiles or thatch. A drone photographs the roof without touching it.

Chichester questions

Can you inspect a listed or thatched roof?

Yes. We only photograph the roof from the air and touch nothing. For thatch we photograph the surface and ridge but do not assess its depth. Any repair rules for a listed building are for your roofer and the council.

Goodwood has an airfield just north of the city, and airspace near airfields has limits. Before each flight the pilot checks the airspace for your address. If a restriction means we cannot fly at that time, we tell you and move the date at no charge.

Is a drone report useful when buying a period home?

Yes. Many buyers book a drone roof inspection before they exchange, so they know what the roof needs before they commit.
